First article: Why do Catholics make the Sign of the Cross?
Version: 1. Date: October 18, 2026. Status: awaiting theological review.

Original prose; no access to or adaptation of A Pocket Guide to the Mass was used.

Sources opened and checked:
- USCCB, Matthew 28:18–20: Jesus commissions Trinitarian Baptism.
- Vatican Catechism, 232–234 and 253–255: one God, three distinct Persons, each fully God.
- Vatican Catechism, 1235: the baptismal cross points to Christ and redemption.
- Vatican Catechism, 2157: daily use of the Sign of the Cross.
- Pope Francis, general audience, April 18, 2018: Baptism, teaching children the gesture, and daily prayer.
- Notre Dame Church Life Journal, The Mass for Millennials: Sign of the Cross: the Latin gesture.
- Metropolitan Cantor Institute of the Byzantine Catholic Archeparchy of Pittsburgh, The Body in Worship: right-to-left Byzantine practice.

The article carries the exact source URLs. The optional deeper resources are the papal audience and the Catechism's Trinity treatment. Printed parent references include paragraph and verse numbers and resource names, so the lesson's explanation and discussion remain usable offline.

Editorial distinctions: the Trinity and Christ's saving death and Resurrection are Catholic teaching. The hand and shoulder order described is a liturgical tradition, not a universal rule for all Catholic Churches. No historical origin story is asserted. ## Revision 2: human dignity and Christ’s love

Added “A reminder that you matter” to the shared explanation, with Catechism 1701–1703 (God’s image in every person), 604–605 and 609 (God’s prior love and Christ’s free sacrifice for all), and Romans 5:8 (Christ died for us while we were sinners). All three source pages were opened on the Vatican and USCCB websites and checked against the claims.

The connection to remembering our worth while making the gesture is presented as a grounded spiritual reflection, not a separate official definition of the gesture. Human worth is rooted in God’s gift, not achievement or deserving salvation. The parent prompts and printed references were updated with the same theme. The three-question structure is preserved.
