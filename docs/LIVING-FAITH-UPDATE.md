# Living the Faith and the Ascension

October 24, 2026. This batch fills the planned items in Parts 1 and 2. Part 3, The Church, is intentionally left for the next pass.

## Published drafts and sources checked

- **The Ascension of Jesus** (the-ascension-of-jesus): Acts 1:1–14 and Catechism 659–664. The risen Christ enters the Father’s glory in his humanity, reigns, and intercedes. Distinguish Ascension from Resurrection and Pentecost, without describing heaven as outer space.
- **Faith, works, and repentance** (faith-works-and-repentance): Ephesians 2:4–10, James 2:14–18, Catechism 1987–1995 and 1996–2002. Grace comes first and enables free cooperation; works are neither a purchase price for salvation nor irrelevant to living faith. Repentance means turning from sin toward God.
- **Baptism and sanctifying grace** (baptism-and-sanctifying-grace): Romans 6:3–11, Catechism 1262–1274, and the USCCB Sacraments and Sacramentals overview. Baptism gives new life and incorporates us into Christ and the Church. Distinguish original sin from a baby’s personal acts; do not promise that all struggle ends after Baptism. Introduce the seven sacraments without trying to replace preparation for each one.
- **Prayer, faith, hope, and love** (prayer-faith-hope-and-love): Matthew 6:5–13, Catechism 2558–2565 and 1812–1829. Explain prayer and the theological virtues in ordinary language, distinguishing hope from a guarantee that every wish comes true.
- **Following Christ in daily choices** (following-christ-in-daily-choices): Matthew 22:34–40, Catechism 2052–2074 and 1783–1785. The commandments belong to love of God and neighbor; conscience requires formation through Scripture, prayer, and the Church’s teaching. Keep examples age-appropriate and avoid creating scrupulous fear.

All cited pages were opened during preparation. The Vatican Ascension page contains 659–664; the article cites that range rather than attributing the separate In Brief paragraphs to it. Sources are linked in the actual article and family reference lists. This source check is not a personal or qualified theological review. All five remain Drafted, version 1, dated October 24, 2026; no ecclesiastical approval is claimed.
