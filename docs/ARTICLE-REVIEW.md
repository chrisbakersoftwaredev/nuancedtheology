# Article review workflow

All articles are currently Drafted. Automated tests and this technical audit do not constitute personal, editorial, theological, or external content review. No ecclesiastical approval is claimed.

## Status definitions

- **Drafted:** written, but no completed personal content review is recorded.
- **Editorially Reviewed:** a named person checked clarity, readability, structure, examples, and reference presentation. This does not assert doctrinal review.
- **Theologically Reviewed:** a named reviewer examined doctrinal accuracy and the cited support for the identified version. Record the scope and unresolved questions.
- **Externally Reviewed:** an identified appropriate qualified person reviewed the identified version. Record qualifications, scope, and permission to identify them. This does not itself mean diocesan approval or an imprimatur.

Article.revision records the status. A non-draft record requires reviewer, reviewedOn (YYYY-MM-DD), reviewedVersion, and note. Increment the article version when its content changes, preserve review history in Git or a separate dated review note, and obtain a fresh review before attributing the old review to the new version. A mismatched review version displays Drafted. A single current stage is a summary, not proof that every other review stage occurred; preserve the evidence and scope in the review note. Do not upgrade a status because a link or build test passed.

- [ ] The article answers its central question and belongs in its assigned chapter.
- [ ] Each doctrinal claim reflects Catholic teaching; distinguish doctrine, custom, and practical suggestions.
- [ ] Scripture quotations match the named translation and verse range; interpretation respects context.
- [ ] Catechism paragraph numbers and linked pages directly support the claim.
- [ ] Historical assertions have reliable supporting sources and appropriate qualifications.
- [ ] External links work, are relevant, and do not imply approval of this article.
- [ ] Beginner vocabulary is clear and necessary theological terms are explained.
- [ ] Examples and analogies help without changing the doctrine or encouraging a mistaken inference.
- [ ] Grace is not portrayed as something earned through unaided effort.
- [ ] Family explanation, parent guidance, memory sentence, questions, practice, and prayer match the article and suit children.
- [ ] Further reading is relevant and clearly distinguished from the original explanation.
- [ ] The next lesson follows naturally; its destination and any planned gaps are correctly described.
- [ ] Mobile headings and paragraphs are readable; sources, family access, and Next are easy to find.
- [ ] A real print preview has no clipping, awkward isolated headings, unexpected blanks, or navigation controls.
- [ ] Record actual review evidence, article version, reviewer, date, and any unresolved issues before changing status.

Review one article at a time. Address blocking doctrinal or source problems before recording a completed review; use a separate change for unrelated curriculum or design work. Technical checks supplement this checklist and do not replace it.
