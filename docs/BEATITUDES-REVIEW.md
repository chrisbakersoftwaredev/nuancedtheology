# Beatitudes editorial review

The main educational areas are Know the Faith, Understanding the Mass, Sacraments, and Living the Faith. Existing URLs continue to serve the same lessons. Sacraments is a main section between Understanding the Mass and Living the Faith. Living the Faith begins with the eight Beatitudes, followed by Everyday Christian Life. Future series appear as planned entries without live links.

Each new article uses the shared lesson layout and has a matching family handout with Read God’s Word, three discussion questions, a weekly practice, and prayer. The family readings and optional longer readings use the local Douay Rheims Challoner text.

## Source basis

- Matthew 5:3–12 provides the eight Beatitudes. Verses 11–12 develop the final Beatitude.
- CCC 1716–1717 describes the Beatitudes as Christ’s portrait and promises of the Kingdom.
- CCC 1718–1719 addresses the desire for happiness.
- CCC 1720–1724 explains divine life as God’s gift and the choices sustained by grace.
- CCC 1725–1729 summarizes the teaching.
- CCC 2544–2547 addresses poverty of heart.
- CCC 1807 addresses justice.
- CCC 2447 addresses the works of mercy.
- CCC 2517–2519 addresses purity of heart.
- CCC 2304–2305 addresses peace and reconciliation in Christ.

All cited paragraphs have official Vatican links in the lessons. New drafts claim no ecclesiastical approval.

## Human review priorities

| Lesson | Additional passage | Review focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poor in spirit | Luke 18:9–14 | Humility, dependence on grace, detachment from possessions, and practical care for people in poverty |
| Those who mourn | John 11:32–36 | Grief, sorrow over sin, the final promise of comfort, and age appropriate support |
| The meek | Matthew 11:28–30 | Gentleness with courage, firm boundaries, and protection from harm |
| Hunger and thirst for righteousness | Matthew 6:31–34 | Holiness and justice together, with the Douay Rheims wording of justice |
| The merciful | Luke 10:33–37 | Mercy, forgiveness, justice, and safety without requiring unsafe reconciliation |
| Pure in heart | Matthew 6:19–23 | Undivided love of God, truth, chastity, dignity, and the gift of seeing God |
| Peacemakers | Matthew 5:23–26 | Reconciliation grounded in justice and care, with children free of responsibility for dangerous adult conflict |
| Persecuted for righteousness | Matthew 5:10–12 | Suffering for fidelity to Christ distinguished from ordinary disagreement or consequences for harmful conduct |

The Douay Rheims numbers the meek at Matthew 5:4 and those who mourn at Matthew 5:5. Many modern editions reverse those two verse numbers. The article quotations and references identify the edition. The series follows the familiar sequence of poor in spirit, mourners, meek, righteousness, mercy, purity, peacemakers, and persecution.

The concrete family activities are pastoral applications of the teaching. A knowledgeable human reviewer should assess their wording and age suitability before publication.
